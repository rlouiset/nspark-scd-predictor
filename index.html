📋 Methodology

Outcome: progression from MDS-UPDRS item 1.1 ≤ 1 (no or only subjective cognitive complaints) to ≥ 2 (clinically evident cognitive impairment), at a given prediction horizon. Both models were trained on the NS-Park cohort (2,593 patients; 21,626 pairs of visits more than one year apart, the earlier visit giving the predictors and the interval the horizon) and applied to PPMI (993 patients) without retraining or recalibration.

Full model: XGBoost classifier (500 trees, depth 15, learning rate 0.01) using all 33 routine variables plus the horizon. Because progressing pairs were up-weighted during training, outputs are recalibrated with Platt scaling fitted on out-of-fold training predictions: p = 1 / (1 + exp(−(−1.2515 + 0.4857 × logit(praw)))). AUC 0.83 (95% CI 0.80–0.86) in the NS-Park test set and 0.76 (0.73–0.79) in PPMI; 0.79 in PPMI patients with ≤ 5 years of disease duration. Missing values follow the default branch learned by XGBoost for each split (Chen & Guestrin, KDD 2016).

Simple model: logistic regression on age, disease duration, education, baseline item 1.1 and horizon (median imputation with missingness indicators, standardisation). AUC 0.79 (0.75–0.83) in NS-Park and 0.76 (0.73–0.79) in PPMI. It performed as well as the full model externally, so most of the predictive information is carried by these few variables.

Feature contributions: for the full model, exact path-dependent SHAP values computed in the browser with the TreeSHAP algorithm (Lundberg et al., Nature Machine Intelligence 2020), multiplied by the Platt slope so that they are expressed on the same calibrated log-odds scale as the displayed risk. For the simple model, each variable's contribution is its term in the regression equation (coefficient × standardised value), which is its exact SHAP value for a linear model (Lundberg & Lee, NeurIPS 2017). In both cases contributions add up exactly to the patient's log-odds minus the model's reference value. The prediction horizon is used by both models but not shown in the chart.

Risk threshold: 22% is the threshold on calibrated probabilities used in the paper for the full model (equivalent to 0.5 before recalibration; sensitivity 0.79, specificity 0.73 in NS-Park). It is close to the overall progression rate in NS-Park (20.9% of visit pairs) and is shown for both models as a reference point, not as a clinically validated cut-off. In PPMI, predicted risks were on average lower than observed (17% vs 28%), so estimates are best read in relative terms in new populations.

Privacy: all computation runs in your browser. No patient data is sent anywhere.

Reference: Louiset R, et al. Predicting Future Cognitive Impairment in Parkinson's Disease From Routine Clinical Assessments. 2026.
